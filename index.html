1. Problem statement

An agency runs several clients from one account. A client is a label on sequences and mailboxes, the only two client-assignable resources (agency-client.ts:7); prospects, tasks, credits and sends have no client dimension (01 §5). So a client cannot see its own prospect's trail without seeing every other client's, no client can be capped, every agency user sees every client, and the two agencies that asked for a restricted client view got hardcoded account ids checked in 19 files (measured, user-details.tsx). Nothing tells the agency any of this; the client finds out when sending stops with no message naming the agency.

What the fix creates. A solo operator with two clients moves from one screen to three places (24 §1.2); if migration is deferred (OQ1) two client models coexist (23 §7.4); "workspace" already means "team" in plan copy (OQ9).

3. What we're building

  1. Nothing changes until the user adds a workspace. The account is the ordinary product. Adding a workspace turns it into an admin panel plus workspaces; the old account becomes the user's own workspace with everything it had.
  2. A workspace is the ordinary product, scoped. Sequences, prospects, mailboxes, settings, integrations and people belong to it; Billing is replaced by Usage; nothing in one workspace is readable from another.
  3. One pool, a cap per workspace, a role per person. Every consumable is metered to the workspace that spent it; a cap stops that resource in that workspace and names the account; Owner and Admin reach every workspace, a Member only theirs; a workspace user holds one of three roles in one workspace.

Invariant in every state: a workspace user never sees another workspace's name, record or count; every client-facing limit message names the account; sending never stops mid-step for a prospect in flight; money shows only on the account's Billing; a client login cannot be left with zero screens.

6. Decisions

D1 – Each client is its own workspace; workspaces never share data. Consequence: the same person in two workspaces is two records; a collision count, never the other record; no moving sequences or prospects between workspaces.

D2 – The admin panel administers; everything operational lives in a workspace, the own outreach included. Admin panel: Home, Workspaces, People, Billing, Analytics. Work queue, Unified Inbox, Tasks, Email Accounts, Templates, API & Integrations on the admin panel are phase 2 (Coming soon). No Settings on the admin panel. Consequence: five clients means five inboxes until phase 2.

D3 – Numbers roll up; records never do. Row roll-ups are phase 2.

D4 – Six fixed roles (§8.5). No per-screen editor. Consequences: a Member sees the whole of every workspace they are on; Team Manager retires; the two hardcoded accounts become Workspace Viewer; at launch every account user is a Member of every workspace until narrowed.

D5 – A workspace user's role is their only permission, one workspace per user. Consequence: a client cannot add its own users.

D6 – Mailboxes are bought inside the workspace that uses them. No pool, assignment or move. Consequence: a mailbox leaves with the client.

D7 – One pool; per workspace and per resource one number. Amended 2 Oct 2026 (phase 1, doc 66 §6, change 93): limits are fixed numbers. For a credit the reservation always equals the limit, so no Reserved column exists anywhere; for emails the number is a monthly limit. Limits never total more than the plan: an edit that would take them over is refused naming both numbers, and a request is approved only when the plan has the amount available. At creation every number comes from one share of the plan (§4 step 4); afterwards each is edited on the workspace's Limits tab or on Billing, or all at once through Change the share. No reserve switch, no bifurcated mode, no independent budget. Consequence: the pool cannot be oversubscribed, so no workspace runs out because another used its share.

D8 – A client sets its own password. Consequence: one forced reset for existing workspace users at launch. Amended 3 Oct 2026 (change 100): creation collects no user. The client's people are invited afterwards, from the workspace's fill-it checklist, the record's Access card or People, each invite carrying one of the three workspace roles.

D9 – Migration moves pointers, not data; 30-day rollback (assumed). Only if OQ1 is "with V1".

D10 – Payment grace: readable, not sending; the client's copy names the account, never the reason.

D11 – Analytics across workspaces ships; API & Integrations on the admin panel does not. Inside a workspace the section stays, scoped. Warm-up stays one TrulyInbox account per account. Consequence: no account-wide API key in phase 1.

D12 – Workspaces, not clients. "Client" only for the outside party. The form does not ask who a workspace is for (OQ12).

D13 – The rail is the product's, flat; the switcher is the tile at its foot. Consequence: from a client workspace the way back is the switcher's account row; only the own workspace has Back to account (OQ13).

D14 – Every workspace is added with its limits reviewed.

D15 – Requests and limit changes are decided on Billing and the record's Limits tab, not on Home.

D16 – Home is four cards and the workspaces table; status is a rule list computed on read (§8.2).

D17 – The record administers: Overview, Members, Limits, Activity. Amended 2 Oct 2026 (change 87): the Email Accounts and Settings Soon tabs are dropped from the record (no promises on a page that administers; both live inside the workspace), and an Activity tab fed only by workspace-feature events is added.

D18 – One period on Home: this cycle to date, compared with last cycle to the same day. No date range on Home; Analytics has one. Consequence: calendar-month reporting lives on Analytics.

D19 – Workspace creation is plan-gated from day one (§5.2), unlimited on paid plans in phase 1. Consequence: the gate's values change by config when the agency plan ships.

D20 – Phase 2: connect an existing account, the mailbox estate, the Work queue, cross-workspace Unified Inbox and Tasks, the client update, an independent budget, a workspace user in two workspaces, per-screen permissions (master PRD, 42).

13. Implementation notes

The mechanism is the prospect gaining a workspace dimension. Everything else scopes resources that already carry a client id.
