1. Problem statement

An agency runs several clients' outreach from one Saleshandy account. It tags each sequence and each mailbox with a client, the only two client-assignable resources (agency-client.ts:7), issues each client a login at /agency/login, and forwards the credentials by hand (01 §1). Every agency user sees every client. Credits, sends and prospects are one pool with no client dimension (01 §5).

The prospect is account-level, so a client cannot be shown its own prospect's email trail without exposing every other client's activity; the sidebar is hidden and the agency screenshares (01 §6a). A sequence's client cannot be changed after activation, so agencies delete and recreate the client (01 §6). No client can be capped, so one client can empty the pool for the rest (Rajveersingh, 10 Aug 2026). Two agencies (measured) that asked for a restricted client view got hardcoded account ids in user-details.tsx, checked in 19 files (measured).

Nothing tells an agency that a client login is a filter over shared data. The client dimension exists in five states across modules (measured, 01 §6). When the pool empties, a client's sending stops with no message naming the agency, so the client blames Saleshandy.

What the fix creates. A solo operator with two clients moves from one screen to three contexts (24 §1.2). If migration is deferred (OQ1), two client models run at once (23 §7.4). "Workspace" already means "team" in plan copy (OQ9). Caps that sum past the pool mean several clients can stop at once.

2. Why workspaces

Four options: patch the client label onto the modules it is missing from, sell a subscription per workspace, split the pool into sub-wallets, or give each client a container on one pool.

CandidateRejected on
Patch the label model: client filter on Prospects and Tasks, per-client capsIsolation and client visibility are mutually exclusive while the prospect is account-level (01 §6a); the hardcoded ids are what patching looks like at scale
A subscription per workspace (Instantly)Contradicts the live "Unlimited Clients" claim on all three tiers ([true,true,true], measured)
Sub-wallets: credits moved out of the poolStrands capacity and forbids the oversubscription agency margin depends on (07 §4.2)
A container per client, one pool, a cap per workspace, a role per person (chosen)HeyReach's shape, the only one compatible with our pricing page (03 §2a)

Not claimed: any competitor's isolation guarantee, which is marketing copy we could not test (03). The competitive bar is named roles plus membership per client and a capped shared pool; nobody verifiable ships a per-screen editor for clients (51 §4).

3. What we're building

  1. Nothing changes until the user adds a workspace. The account is the ordinary product with one line under the onboarding checklist. Adding a workspace turns the account into an admin panel plus workspaces, and the old account becomes the user's own workspace with everything it had.
  2. A workspace is the ordinary product, scoped. Its sequences, prospects, mailboxes, settings, integrations and people belong to it; Billing is replaced by Usage; nothing in one workspace is readable from another.
  3. One pool, a cap per workspace, a role per person. Every consumable is metered to the workspace that spent it; reaching a cap stops that resource in that workspace and names the account; Owner and Admin reach every workspace, a Member only the workspaces they are on; a workspace user holds one of three roles in one workspace.

Invariant in every state: a workspace user never sees another workspace's name, record or count; every client-facing limit message names the account; sending never stops mid-step for a prospect in flight; money is visible only on the account's Billing; a client login cannot be left with zero screens.

4. User stories

US-1 – I add a workspace from where I already am. As the owner of an ordinary account, I want to add a workspace without losing anything I had, and undo it while the new workspace is the only other one, so that becoming an agency is a step, not a migration.

US-2 – I see what each workspace costs and returns. As an Owner, I want to see what each workspace costs to serve and rank workspaces by outcome, so that I price and prune on numbers.

US-3 – I only see the workspaces I am on. As a Member, I want to see and act on only my workspaces, so that I cannot touch a workspace I do not run.

US-4 – I read my own prospect's trail. As a Workspace Admin or Collaborator, I want to open my prospect and read its full email trail, so that the agency never has to screenshare.

US-5 – I see everything and change nothing. As a Workspace Viewer, I want every screen read-only and one plain statement of my role, so that I am never surprised by a dead control.

US-6 – I cap a workspace and approve more in one action. As an Owner or Admin, I want to cap each workspace's usage, be asked when a client wants more, and approve in one action.

US-7 – A mailbox belongs to the workspace that bought it. As an Owner, Admin or Member, I want to buy each mailbox inside the workspace that sends from it, so that ownership is never ambiguous.

US-8 – Launch day loses nothing. As an existing account, I want every client I have to become a workspace on launch day, with nothing I could see yesterday gone.

US-9 – I restrict staff to their workspaces. As an Owner, I want to decide which staff see which workspace.

US-10 – My own outreach is a workspace too. As an account, I want to run my own outreach in a workspace of its own, on the same pool, under its own cap.

Phase 2: As a Member, I work one queue across my workspaces. In phase 1 each workspace has its own inbox and tasks, and Home carries per-workspace counts.

5. Decisions

D1 – Each client is its own workspace; workspaces never share data. The prospect gains a workspace dimension, the real cost of this project (OQ6). Consequence to accept explicitly: the same person in two workspaces is two records; the account sees a collision count, never the other record; moving a sequence or a prospect between workspaces is not in this release.

D2 – The admin panel administers; everything operational lives in a workspace, the account's own outreach included. The admin panel is Home, Workspaces, People, Billing, Analytics. Work queue, Unified Inbox, Tasks, Email Accounts, Templates and API & Integrations on the admin panel are phase 2, shown as Coming soon. There is no Settings on the admin panel: every setting lives in a workspace. Consequence to accept explicitly: an operator with five clients answers replies in five inboxes until phase 2; Home's per-workspace counts are the only cross-workspace view of work.

D3 – Numbers roll up; records never do. Analytics blends across workspaces with a Workspace filter and column. A sequence, a prospect, a mailbox never leaves its workspace; no screen lists them across workspaces. Row roll-ups of replies and tasks are phase 2.

D4 – Six fixed roles. Account: Owner, Admin, Member. Workspace: Workspace Admin, Collaborator, Viewer. The table in Screen 6 is the contract. No per-screen editor, preset or Custom state in phase 1. Every recorded client-side ask is one of three shapes (Client Permissions, 51). Consequences to accept explicitly: a Member sees the whole of every workspace they are on; Team Manager retires into Member plus Admin; the two hardcoded accounts become Workspace Viewer; on launch day every account user is a Member of every workspace until narrowed.

D5 – A workspace user's role is their only permission, set per person, one workspace per user. Full access becomes Workspace Admin, Limited becomes Collaborator, No access becomes Viewer, which gains read access to the whole workspace. Consequence to accept explicitly: a client cannot add its own users; every workspace user is invited by the account.

D6 – Mailboxes are bought inside the workspace that uses them and belong to it from purchase. No pool, assignment or move in phase 1. Consequence to accept explicitly: a mailbox bought for a client that leaves is released with the client, not reclaimed.

D7 – One pool; per workspace and per resource a cap and an optional reservation. A cap can exceed the pool, a reservation cannot. No bifurcated mode and no independent budget in phase 1. Consequence to accept explicitly: every workspace draws from the shared pool; the pool can run out while every workspace is under cap, and Billing must say so.

D8 – A client sets its own password; the account never sees it. Invite-and-accept replaces forwarded credentials. Consequence to accept explicitly: every existing workspace user gets one forced password reset on launch.

D9 – Migration moves pointers, not data; 30-day rollback (assumed). Sequences and mailboxes follow their client; prospects follow their sequence; anything on no client or two goes to a review. Ships only if OQ1 resolves to "with V1".

D10 – During the account's payment grace period a workspace stays readable and stops sending. The client's copy names the account, never the reason.

D11 – Analytics across workspaces ships; API & Integrations on the admin panel does not. Inside each workspace the existing API & Integrations section stays, scoped to it; an existing key with no workspace acts in the account's own workspace. Warm-up stays one TrulyInbox account per account. Consequence to accept explicitly: no account-wide API key in phase 1.

D12 – The product says workspaces, not clients. "Client" only where it means the outside party: View as client, the sign-in orientation line, the migration screen. The form does not ask who a workspace is for; every added workspace uses client wording (OQ12).

D13 – The rail is the product's rail, flat, and the switcher is the tile at its foot. Adding a workspace adds items to the rail (Home, Workspaces, People, Billing, Analytics on the account; Usage in a workspace); it never adds a heading or a second navigation. A workspace user's tile is static. Consequence to accept explicitly: the way from a client workspace back to the admin panel is the switcher's account row; only the account's own workspace carries a Back to account item (OQ13).

D14 – Every workspace is added with its limits reviewed. Add a workspace is Details then Limits; Limits lists every resource the plan holds with a suggested cap per row; Add is on step 2. Consequence to accept explicitly: adding a workspace is two screens, not one; a workspace can never exist with unreviewed caps.

D15 – Requests and limit changes are decided on Billing > Workspace limits & usage and on the record's Limits tab, not on Home. Home's status line names what waits ("8 decisions are waiting", illustrative) and links to Billing. Consequence to accept explicitly: an Owner reads Home, then goes one screen further to act.

D16 – Home is four hero cards and the workspaces table, nothing else. Workspaces, Active sequences, Outreach this cycle, Replies this cycle; the table keeps its columns in every state, with a Setup pending chip for a workspace that has steps left. Consequence to accept explicitly: plan pacing and pooled-resource runway live only on Billing; a fresh account's Home is mostly empty until a workspace sends.

D17 – The workspace record administers: Overview, Members, Limits. Its Email Accounts and Settings tabs are Coming soon in phase 1; mailboxes and settings are managed inside the workspace, one click away. Consequence to accept explicitly: the read-only record view of a workspace's mailboxes is phase 2.

D18 – Connect an existing account, the mailbox estate, the Work queue, the cross-workspace Unified Inbox and Tasks, the client update, an independent budget per workspace, a workspace user in two workspaces and per-screen permissions are phase 2. Specified in the master PRD and 42. Nothing in them ships in phase 1.

!The ordinary account: Sequences with the onboarding checklist and, under it, the one line that adds a workspace.

The ordinary account: Sequences with the onboarding checklist and, under it, the one line that adds a workspace. #setup · #confirm · #transform

What it's for. Every account before it has a second workspace. The Owner arrives from Sequences, where the product lands today.

What it must do:

  • Be the product as it is today: the same rail (Sequences to Analytics, plus People and Billing), the same Sequences page with its onboarding checklist, no page header, no switcher tile.
  • Carry one line under the checklist, never a counted step, in the checklist's own type: "Running outreach for more than one business, or for clients? Give each its own workspace: its own sequences, prospects and mailboxes, separate from yours and from each other. Add a workspace →"
  • Open a confirmation on the first Add a workspace, the design system's Medium modal: "Add a workspace?", one sentence, a picture of your workspace today beside a new empty one, one line that it can be undone, Not now and the primary action. Nothing changes until it is confirmed.
  • On confirm, play the transformation and open the Add a workspace modal (Screen 3). When the workspace exists, the account is multi-workspace: Home, Workspaces, People, Billing and Analytics appear in the rail, the switcher tile appears at its foot, and the previous account is the user's own workspace with everything it had.
  • Return the account to the single-workspace state when the only other workspace is deleted (the undo the confirmation promises).

Outcomes this screen must guarantee:

  • An account that never adds a workspace never sees the word "workspace" outside the checklist line.
  • An account created as an agency from signup skips the confirmation (assumed; not drawn).

19. Implementation notes

The mechanism behind Screen 8 is the prospect gaining a workspace dimension. Everything else is scoping over resources that already carry a client id. Outcome only: a prospect belongs to exactly one workspace; the same email in two workspaces is two prospects; the account sees the fact and count of a collision, never the other record. Existing prospects are assigned by their sequence at conversion; the rest enter the review. Existing API consumers sending no workspace are pinned to the account's own workspace (08 §12).
