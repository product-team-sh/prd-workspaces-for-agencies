Workspaces for Agencies, Phase 1 PRD

Design reference: Prototype vC

1. Purpose

Let an agency manage its own outreach and each client’s outreach separately in one Saleshandy account. Each workspace contains its own prospects, sequences, email accounts, tasks, inbox, templates, settings, integrations, API keys and users.

The first workspace converts the account into an Admin Panel plus workspaces. The account’s existing data becomes the agency’s own workspace without being changed. Client workspaces remain isolated from one another.

Success criteria

  • No cross-workspace data or access leaks.
  • Existing outreach continues safely during conversion and workspace changes.
  • Agencies can manage client access and allocate usage limits from one place.
  • Client users can work only in workspaces they belong to and never see the agency’s plan, prices or other clients.
  • Teams can understand and recover from failed creation, conversion or limit changes without creating duplicates or leaving the account in a partial state.

2. Phase 1 scope

Included: first-workspace conversion; create, disable, delete and restore workspaces; workspace switching; roles and invitations; shared usage limits and requests for more capacity; Admin Panel dashboard and workspaces list; workspace records; People; Billing; cross-workspace Analytics; Whitelabel; client Usage & billing; workspace-level client invitations; required audit events and rollout monitoring.

Not included: connecting an existing account to a workspace model; cross-workspace Work queue, Unified Inbox or Tasks; shared email-account management across workspaces; independent client budgets; automatic limit allocation or auto-give policies; approval/caps for client email-account purchases; per-screen permissions; per-client branding; row roll-ups. These are phase 2 unless listed as a dependency below.

The legacy Client Management experience and the 2022 Agency Portal are not converted. An account can use either legacy Client Management or Workspaces, not both.

3. Product model and access principles

3.1 Workspace model

  • Before the first workspace is added, the product stays as it is today, except for approved workspace entry points.
  • After conversion, the Admin Panel contains Dashboard, Workspaces, People, Usage & limits, Billing, Analytics and Whitelabel. Whitelabel is Account Owner-only.
  • The original account becomes the agency’s own workspace. Existing records stay there and must match pre-conversion counts by record type.
  • Each client workspace has its own data, users and usage limits. The same email address in two workspaces represents two separate prospects. The account may see that a duplicate exists and its count, but never the other workspace’s prospect record.
  • A workspace’s ordinary product screens remain familiar, but all data and actions are scoped to that workspace. Workspace Billing is replaced by Usage & billing.

3.2 Invariants

  1. A client user never sees another workspace’s name, record, count, users or other private data.
  2. An Account Member sees only workspaces assigned to them.
  3. The server enforces every permission; hiding a control is not sufficient.
  4. Client-facing limit, pause and blocking messages name the agency, not its plan or internal account state.
  5. Client users never see prices, invoices, plan details or the agency’s other clients.
  6. A resource reaching its limit stops that resource only. An in-flight sequence step completes; no prospect is sent twice or skipped. Other channels, replies and tasks continue unless separately blocked.
  7. A disabled workspace pauses its sequences but keeps its data. Its limits continue to count against the plan.
  8. No client login is left with zero accessible screens.

4. Roles and permissions

There are six fixed roles. No per-screen permission editor is in scope. The referenced role matrix remains the authority for individual Settings pages and module-level permissions: role and permission matrix.

Additional rules:

  • Account Owners and Account Admins act as Workspace Admin in every workspace.
  • An Account Member has a separate workspace role in each assigned workspace and must have at least one workspace.
  • A client user can belong to multiple workspaces under one sign-in, but the agency adds them to additional workspaces.
  • A client Workspace Admin can invite users into their own workspace only.
  • Invited users default to Workspace Collaborator on every invitation path.
  • An email address can be either an agency user or a client user, never both. An address that owns another Saleshandy account cannot be invited.
  • Only an Account Owner can delete a workspace. An Account Owner or Account Admin can disable one.
  • Multiple Account Owners are allowed. Ownership changes require both parties to confirm by email. The last Owner cannot be removed or demoted.
  • Invitees set their own passwords. Invites expire after 14 days and can be resent.

Sign-in destinations

  • Account Owner / Account Admin: Admin Panel Dashboard.
  • Account Member: Workspaces list showing only assigned workspaces.
  • Client user with one workspace: that workspace’s Sequences page, with no switcher.
  • Client user with multiple workspaces: last-used workspace; switcher lists only their workspaces.
  • A permitted deep link opens the requested screen after sign-in. An unauthorized link shows an access message and a route back to the user’s landing page.

5. Plan capacity, limits and requests

5.1 Plan eligibility

Workspace creation is controlled by a configurable workspace_allowance per plan, excluding the agency’s own workspace.

  • Phase 1 proposal: trial and free plans have an allowance of 0; paid plans have unlimited workspaces.
  • Intended later model: a finite allowance for an agency plan and 0 for other plans.
  • If the allowance is 0, hide Add a workspace and refuse deep links with an explanation and plan link.
  • At the allowance, disable Add a workspace and link to Billing to add capacity.
  • If an account downgrades below its current workspace count, existing workspaces continue working, but no new workspace can be added until the account is within its allowance.
  • Eligible accounts: new accounts and existing accounts without Client Management clients, subject to plan allowance. Accounts with one or more Client Management clients and accounts on the 2022 Agency Portal are excluded. After the last legacy client is deleted, eligibility is reevaluated.

5.2 Resources and proposed plan values

The following values were carried over from the 6 October PRD and must be verified against current pricing before release.

Dialer creditsPurchased add-onInclude only when the account has a balance; billing cadence needs confirmation

Rules:

  • Each workspace has a fixed limit per resource, all drawn from one account-level pool.
  • Available = plan capacity minus all amounts allocated to workspaces. Left = a workspace’s limit minus its consumption.
  • The sum allocated across workspaces can never exceed available plan capacity. Apply the same rule to emails and credits.
  • Credits are reserved when allocated. A workspace may consume up to its limit. When used up, only that resource stops.
  • A limit below current usage is allowed only after clearly explaining what will stop. The confirmation action must name the consequence, such as “Save and stop sending.”
  • Disabled workspaces continue to reserve their limits. Capacity is released only by lowering limits.
  • Prospect storage, email accounts, active prospects and seats are not usage-limit rows. Seat treatment for client users needs final confirmation.
  • The agency’s own workspace uses the same pool. After the first client workspace is added, the Owner or Admin must set its limits. Until then, sequences use what remains available and cannot consume client allocations. The Account Member experience while limits are unset needs confirmation.

5.3 Creating a workspace’s limits

  • The creation form defaults to allocating 10% of currently available capacity for each resource, rounded down to a whole unit. A share of 100% allocates all currently available capacity.
  • Users can edit individual values instead of using the share slider. Returning to slider mode recalculates every value from the selected share.
  • Zero is allowed and means the resource is unavailable in that workspace.
  • Values must be whole, non-negative numbers. Accept digits with optional thousands separators; reject decimals, negatives and letters rather than silently stripping characters.
  • Revalidate all numbers against current server-side availability at submit. If capacity changed while the form was open, refresh slider values or flag manually entered amounts and explain the change.

5.4 Requests for more capacity

  • Workspace Admins and Collaborators can submit one request containing one or more resources and amounts, with a required reason. Viewers cannot request.
  • The requester can withdraw only their own request while it is waiting.
  • Requests appear in one shared request card reached from Usage & limits, Dashboard and Workspaces banners, the bell, the workspace record and client Usage & billing. All entry points open the same request; there must not be separate decision states.
  • Account Owners and Account Admins can give, partially give or decline with an optional note. When only part can be given, the agency chooses either Keep the remainder waiting or Close the remainder.
  • The requester sees each resource’s outcome, including “Partly answered,” and any decline note.
  • If another administrator has already answered a request, reject the stale action and show who answered and when.
  • Requests and limit changes are allowed during payment grace, but all sending is paused until payment is fixed.

6. Core workflows

6.1 Discover and add the first workspace

Before the first workspace exists, the only new UI is the approved entry points for Account Owners and Account Admins:

  1. Add a workspace CTA and context line in Client Management when the account has no legacy client.
  2. Add a workspace entry in the switcher.
  3. A workspace-first onboarding block on empty Sequences only for accounts confirmed to have identified as an agency at signup. This signal still needs verification.
  4. A deep link to the creation flow, subject to eligibility and plan rules.

Account Members and client users do not see these entry points. Accounts using legacy Client Management cannot access the new flow.

6.2 First-workspace creation and conversion

  1. Show a confirmation explaining that existing work remains in place and that deleting all client workspaces later returns the account to single-account mode. Accounts identified as agencies at signup may skip this confirmation; confirm the signup signal before shipping.
  2. Show a one-step form with workspace name, optional website, icon colour, allocation slider and per-resource limit values.
  3. Validate inputs, plan capacity and available limits on both client and server.
  4. On submit, create the first workspace and convert the account as one atomic operation. Show progress and never expose a half-converted state.
  5. On success, show Enter your workspaces. It opens the Admin Panel Dashboard and starts the setup guide.
  6. On failure or timeout, leave the account exactly as it was, keep entered values and offer Retry. The timeout limit is not final; 60 seconds is the current assumption.

Conversion must preserve the account’s existing sequences, prospects, email accounts, templates, tasks, inbox threads, settings, schedules, fields, integrations, API keys, webhooks and Do Not Contact entries in the agency’s own workspace. Before/after record counts must match. Running sequences keep their schedule and do not skip or double-send emails. API keys continue to work and default to the own workspace. Plan, Billing and Whitelabel remain unchanged.

Existing teammate role mapping during conversion needs engineering confirmation. The proposed mapping is Owner to Account Owner, Admin to Account Admin, and Member to Account Member on the own workspace at Workspace Collaborator. The confirmation must disclose any access widening.

6.3 Add later workspaces

Later workspaces do not need the confirmation or conversion animation. Create the workspace with its limits and no client user, show a brief success message, then open its Sequences page with a setup checklist: connect an email account, add prospects, create a sequence, launch it and invite client users. If the workspace is created but saving its limits fails, it must remain at zero limits and must not be created again on retry.

6.4 Workspace form validation

  • Name: required; 1–100 visible characters; trim leading/trailing spaces and collapse internal whitespace; allow printable characters, international scripts, punctuation and emoji; unique across active and recently deleted workspaces within the account, case-insensitively after normalisation. The 100-character limit and character-count method need confirmation.
  • Website: optional. Accept a hostname with or without scheme; store a lowercase hostname only, remove www., path, query, fragment and port; open with HTTPS. Require a valid hostname with a dotted domain; phase 1 refuses IP addresses and non-Latin domains.
  • Icon colour: one of six supported swatches; default to the first unused colour, otherwise the first swatch.
  • Limits: whole numbers from zero to currently available capacity.
  • Show field-level errors with corrective wording. Never silently strip invalid numeric input.
  • Prevent duplicate submissions. If the request times out, “Check again” must determine whether creation succeeded and never create a duplicate.

6.5 Setup guide

A learn-by-doing guide starts for the person who created the first workspace. It takes the user through the Dashboard row, inviting client users, setting limits, entering the workspace and switching between the Admin Panel and workspace.

The guide can be skipped at any step, resumed from the profile menu and replayed after completion. Progress is stored per user across refresh, sign-out and devices. Account Members and client users do not see the guide. Preview mode (“View as client”) disables actions that would send or change real data and writes an audit event.

6.6 Disable, delete, revert and restore

  • Disable: Account Owner or Admin. Pause sequences and block client access, but retain data and editable limits. Limits continue counting against the plan.
  • Delete: Account Owner only; the agency’s own workspace cannot be deleted. Confirm with a clear description of consequences. Deleted workspaces go to Recently deleted for 30 days and disappear from all normal lists and switchers. Client users cannot access them.
  • Delete the last client workspace: revert the account to single-account mode. The own workspace’s data becomes the account’s data unchanged; deleted client data is never merged into it. Remove the own workspace’s allocated limits, restore Client Management and remove the Admin Panel. Account Owner/Admin roles map back to the production account roles. Members assigned only to the deleted workspace are disabled; Member role mapping for those assigned to the own workspace follows the production model.
  • Restore: the Account Owner can restore within 30 days. Conversion runs again with the same preservation guarantees. Restore workspace data and limits only up to current available capacity. Client users must be re-invited before regaining access.

7. Navigation and screens

7.1 Global navigation

  • The rail is flat, with the workspace switcher at the bottom. Inside a workspace, show workspace-scoped modules and Usage & billing instead of Billing; People is not shown within a workspace.
  • Switcher order: Admin Panel, agency’s own workspace, then client workspaces alphabetically. Show only a Disabled tag; do not expose counts or roles in switcher rows. Search appears from eight rows.
  • Account Owner/Admin can add workspaces. Account Members see only assigned workspaces and cannot add. A client user with one workspace sees only its name and avatar; a multi-workspace client user’s switcher lists only their workspaces.
  • Switching workspaces preserves the current type of screen and tab where possible. If the target workspace does not support or allow the screen, navigate to its default Sequences page. The Admin row returns to the Admin Panel.
  • The bell lists actionable requests and problems such as paused sending, disconnected email accounts and payment failure. Its count must equal its items.
  • The profile menu uses the production menu and adds Resume guide / Replay guide for Account Owners and Admins only.

7.3 Workspace modules and permissions

Existing modules operate on workspace data, including Sequences, Prospects/CRM, Lead Finder, Unified Inbox, Tasks, Email Verifier, Templates, Inbox Radar, Email Warm-up, Reports, Dialer, API & Integrations and Do Not Contact.

  • Remove client pickers and “Client Associated” filters where workspace context replaces them.
  • Workspace Admins can export; Collaborators and Viewers cannot. Enforce this in the UI and API.
  • Email account access: Viewer can read the list and health; Collaborator can connect; Workspace Admin can connect and buy. Purchases are billed to the agency and show no price to client users. Agency staff may see purchase prices in Usage & billing › Email Account Infrastructure. Phase 1 purchases are uncapped unless the final decision changes.
  • Workspace settings follow the role matrix and account defaults/locks. The account-level Do Not Contact list cannot be removed from inside a workspace; a workspace may add entries.
  • An existing API key without an explicit workspace acts only on the agency’s own workspace.

7.4 Client experience

Client users land on Sequences. They see only the workspace(s) they belong to. Usage & billing has Limits and Email Account Infrastructure tabs, current usage and relevant status messages. Admins and Collaborators can Request more, with at least one amount and a required reason; Viewers see the information read-only. A usage report downloaded by a client covers only that workspace.

When a resource is near its limit (90% or more), used up, or an email account is disconnected, show a clear message that names the agency. On email limit exhaustion, sending pauses but replies continue.

7.5 Client invitations

A Workspace Admin can invite and manage users inside their own workspace through Settings › Users & Teams. The workspace is fixed in the invite flow; one role is chosen for a batch. Collaborators and Viewers are read-only. Client Workspace Admins cannot invite agency users, invite someone already attached to another workspace from this screen, or change/remove their own access.

Invitations accept up to 50 addresses per send. Duplicates collapse. Every address not invited is named with a reason, both before sending where possible and in the completion message if skipped by the server. Changes to invitations and roles appear immediately in People, Members, the person page and Activity.

8. Standard product behaviour

The prototype defines layout, copy and flow–not behaviour that is missing from the prototype. The following requirements apply to every new and changed screen.

Navigation and state

  • Every screen, tab and record has a stable URL. Query parameters preserve filters, sorting, search, selected tabs and Dashboard period/toggle.
  • Browser Back returns to the previous view, tab and scroll position. Back closes an open drawer/dialog before leaving the page.
  • Refresh preserves the current view and data; in-progress form values survive refresh for the session.
  • Unauthorized or removed routes show a clear no-access state without revealing protected names or data.
  • A change made on one screen updates all other surfaces from the same source of truth. Concurrent updates appear promptly. Stale actions are refused with the current state; failed optimistic updates roll back with an explanation.

Forms and actions

  • Trim and normalise before validation and saving. Validate on blur after entry and on submit; show errors next to the field and focus the first invalid field on submit.
  • Prevent double submits. Disabled controls explain why. Use whole-number validation and formatted thousands separators; never silently strip invalid input.
  • Server-side validation repeats client-side rules.
  • Warn before discarding unsaved changes. Save is enabled only when something changed and updates only changed fields.
  • Destructive actions require confirmation that names the action and consequence. Consequential changes produce an Undo receipt when possible; Undo restores all affected values and screens.

Dialogs, loading and errors

  • Dialogs and drawers have a title, close control, Escape handling and correct keyboard focus. Avoid stacking dialogs except for an action confirmation.
  • Lists and panels have loading, empty and error states. Errors provide Retry; one failed panel must not blank the page. Never leave an indefinite spinner.
  • Toasts with Undo stay visible for at least six seconds (target), pause on hover and restore every value changed by the action.

Accessibility and layout

  • All flows work by keyboard with visible focus. Controls have accessible names; statuses are not communicated by colour alone.
  • Meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast. Support viewports from 390px to 1280px without page-level horizontal overflow; tables may scroll within their panel or stack on small screens.
  • Format numbers with thousands separators, money in the account currency, dates/times in the viewer’s time zone and long names/emails with ellipses plus a way to see the full value.

9. Engineering, security and data

Required engineering controls

  • Add a workspace dimension to prospects and scope every data read/write to a workspace. All existing records belong to exactly one workspace after conversion.
  • Meter resource use to the workspace that consumed it. Dashboard, usage tables and status indicators must derive from shared per-workspace aggregates; target freshness is 15 minutes, subject to confirmation. Avoid per-workspace query fan-out on page load.
  • Use atomic conversion with support-readable before/after counts for every migrated record type. If conversion fails, the account remains unchanged. Confirm conversion duration against the largest accounts.
  • Never rely on UI-only gating. Enforce membership, roles, export restrictions, plan capacity, invite restrictions and limits in server/API responses.
  • Do not include another workspace’s names, records, counts or users in client/Account Member responses, errors, autocomplete or search results.
  • Attribute every write to the workspace of the action, not a session-level “current workspace”.
  • Audit workspace entry, limit changes, capacity decisions, exports, View as client, invitations, role changes, disable/delete/restore and relevant sign-ins.
  • Do not send prospect email addresses, message bodies, client-user names or invitee addresses in analytics events.

10. Instrumentation, rollout and success

Events

Instrument at least: successful/failed conversion; account revert; workspace create/blocked/failed; guide steps/skip/resume/completion; client session and blocked navigation; role changes; invites and reasons not sent; limit set/used up; capacity requested/resolved; prospect trail viewed; membership narrowed; and View as client. Keep event properties aggregate or ID-only as appropriate; exclude personal content and email addresses.

Rollout

  1. Internal stage: Saleshandy’s own account with three fixture workspaces. All P0 cases pass; named sign-off is required for conversion integrity and the prospect trail isolation check.
  2. Limited stage: the interviewed agency, if willing, plus the two hardcoded accounts. Monitor for 14 days; keep leak and conversion guardrails clean; remove the Workspace Viewer hardcode.
  3. General stage: new signups and eligible existing accounts after the limited-stage gates pass.

Release blockers and guardrails

  • Any confirmed cross-workspace leak: halt the stage and roll back exposure.
  • Any conversion with unequal before/after record counts: halt conversions and restore the affected account.
  • Conversion failures above 2% of daily attempts (assumption): halt new conversions.
  • Monitor multiple simultaneous client stops across accounts and halt expansion if the defined threshold is exceeded.

Success measures

Non-negotiable at day 90: zero confirmed data leaks; zero conversions with unequal record counts; both hardcoded accounts use Workspace Viewer instead of the old hardcode.

Early indicators to review by day 14: successful first-attempt conversion rate; client second-session rate; blocked navigation per client session; proportion of workspaces with edited limits; membership narrowing; guide completion; and capacity requests decided within 24 hours. The source targets are assumptions and should be confirmed before becoming launch gates. The principal proposed targets are: >99% first-attempt conversions (investigate below 97%), >50% client second sessions (investigate below 30%), >40% guide completion (investigate below 20%), and >70% of requests decided in 24 hours (investigate below 40%).
