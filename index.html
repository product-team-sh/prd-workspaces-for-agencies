1. Problem

What users do. An agency runs cold outreach for several clients from one account. It tags each sequence with a client in Sequence → Settings and each mailbox in that mailbox's General Settings, the only two client-assignable resource types (agency-client.ts:7); issues each client a login at /agency/login, default LimitedAccess; and forwards the emailed credentials by hand (01 §1). Every agency user sees every client. Credits, sends and prospects are one account pool with no client dimension (01 §5).

What actually happens. The prospect is account-level: clientAssociated is an array derived from the sequences it sits on, not an assignment (01 §6a). A client therefore cannot be shown its own prospect's email trail without exposing that prospect's activity for every other client, so the sidebar is hidden and the agency screenshares. A sequence's client cannot be changed after activation (product copy: "it cannot be unlinked to prevent potential data mismatches and reporting discrepancies"), so agencies delete and recreate the client, which has already left a live sequence pointing at a deleted client (01 §6). No client can be capped, so one client can empty the pool for the rest (Rajveersingh, 10 Aug 2026). Two agencies (measured) that asked for a restricted client view got it as hardcoded account ids 190877 and 197372 in user-details.tsx, checked in 19 files (measured). A third agency cannot have it.

Why they do not find out. Nothing tells an agency that a client login is a filter over shared data rather than a container. The client dimension exists in five states (measured): assignable and filterable on Sequences and Email Accounts; a derived column that cannot be filtered on Prospects (prospect-list.tsx:1663); neither shown nor filterable on Tasks (tasks-content.tsx:2366); filterable in Unified Inbox; absent from Dialer (zero client references under src/components/dialer/, measured). When the pool empties, a client's sending stops with no message naming the agency, so the client blames Saleshandy.

What the fix creates. A solo operator with two clients moves from one screen to three or four contexts (24 §1.2), and that group is unsized. If migration is deferred (OQ1), two client models run in production at once (23 §7.4). "Workspace" already means "team" in plan copy (compare-plans-config.ts:147). Caps that sum past the pool mean several clients can stop at once, arriving as several tickets (07 §8.5).

3. What we're building

  1. A client is a workspace. Its sequences, prospects, mailboxes, settings and members belong to it; nothing in one workspace is readable from another; the master account administers workspaces and runs no outreach of its own.
  2. One pool, a cap per workspace. Every consumable is metered to the workspace that spent it; reaching a cap stops that workspace, names the agency, and offers request-more; the agency may cap past the pool and may never reserve past it.
  3. Access is set per workspace and membership is the primitive. A person sees only the workspaces they are a member of; a client reaches only the screens and actions its agency granted; replies and tasks roll up across a person's assigned workspaces with the client on every row; records never roll up.

Invariant in every state: a client user never sees another workspace's name, record or count; every client-facing limit message names the agency; sending never stops mid-step for a prospect already in flight; an action taken from a roll-up row writes into that row's workspace; a client login cannot be left with zero screens.

5. Decisions

D1 – A client is a multi-workspace container, not a filter. Hard data isolation; every scope in 20 §1 resolves by three tests: whose records (per workspace), who pays (pool at the account, cap per workspace), must one person act across clients (roll-up by assignment). Why: the label model has a ceiling set by the account-level prospect, and no permission model lifts it (01 §6a). Cost: the prospect gains a workspace dimension, which is the whole cost of the project and is unpriced (OQ10). Consequence to accept explicitly: the same person in two workspaces is two records. Cross-client de-duplication becomes a count the agency sees, never the records (Pattern B, 08 §7). Moving a sequence or a prospect between workspaces is out of V1.

D2 – The master account manages other accounts and nothing else; the agency's own outreach is a peer workspace. Why: letting the agency run campaigns from the workspace it administers clients from means every screen answers "whose work is this" before it answers anything else, and the answer differs per row. The distinction moves into the switcher once instead of onto eleven screens (22 §6.1). Cost: every agency gains one context; a solo operator with two clients gains two or three (24 §1.2). Consequence to accept explicitly: the rule that ships is "the master holds what the account owns and no single workspace does": Dashboard, Work queue, Client Management, Billing, the mailbox estate, the template library and the two roll-ups. The own-outreach workspace draws on the pool under its own cap.

D2a – The own-outreach workspace is a peer in the roll-ups, not only in allocation. Closes OQ12. Its replies appear in the master's roll-up, named as the agency, wherever a portfolio view exists. Why: it was included in allocation and excluded from the roll-ups, which is inconsistent, and by D3 replies are work. Excluding them meant the one workspace whose replies nobody else will chase was the one left out of the chase list. Consequence to accept explicitly: the roll-up is no longer purely client work, so its header cannot say "across your clients" without qualification. An operator sees no own-workspace rows, because an operator has no portfolio view to put them in.

D3 – Work rolls up, records do not, in three tiers. Work items (replies, tasks) roll up in full, filtered by membership, client on every row. Asset counts and rates roll up on the Dashboard. Asset records (sequences, prospects, mailboxes) never cross a workspace (16 §0.1, 24 §3.1). Why: a number is not a record; a list filtered by assignment with the client named on each row is safe, and 3 of 3 verifiable competitors ship the inbox this way. Consequence to accept explicitly: the Replies roll-up must not regress what the Unified Inbox client filter does today; the Tasks roll-up is net new. The Dialer has no workspace to land a call record in today, so the roll-up cannot include calls until it does (§9).

D4 – Assignment is the primitive; five roles. Account owner, Manager, Operator, and two client roles (16 §1). Membership per workspace is the new axis; billing visibility is a switch per agency member, not a rung. Why: every cross-client list becomes safe by one filter, and the interviewed agency wants staff restriction and cannot get it. Consequence to accept explicitly: on launch day every agency user is a member of every workspace, so the filter is empty until an agency narrows it. M1 headlines narrowing; nothing forces it.

D5 – Access is set per client workspace: 7 screens, 4 actions, presets. Screens: Sequences, Replies, Prospects, Lead Finder, Email accounts, Reports, Usage. Presets: Reports only, Lead Finder only, Read everything, Reply and run, Everything (22 §7.2). An action cannot be granted without the screen it acts on. The last screen cannot be switched off, because Disable already exists for suspension. Set by owner or manager, never an operator. Why: the product already hardcodes the first two presets for two paying accounts; the 321 permissions exist and lack a per-workspace surface. Consequence to accept explicitly: the old Full / Limited / No access levels survive only as presets.

D5a – The access set is the only permission system for a client user. Closes OQ8. A client user has no role of their own: what they can reach is the workspace's, set once. Why: two controls over one outcome disagree, and they did. The invite quoted the per-user role while the client landed on the access set, so Brightline was invited with "See everything of your own, including the full email trail, and reply" and met one screen and no reply. The access set wins because it is the one the client can be shown. Consequence to accept explicitly: two people from the same client cannot be given different access. If an agency asks for that, the answer is a second workspace, not a second role. Invite copy is derived from the access set, so it cannot promise what the workspace does not grant. Agency staff keep their roles, which are a different axis.

D5b – The four actions are Reply, Pause and edit, Export, Reveal leads. Closes OQ9. Invite-colleagues is removed. Why: inviting a colleague is administration, not outreach, and the one action that spends the agency's money directly, revealing a lead, was absent. That absence is why the Lead Finder only preset had to be labelled view-only: the product could grant the screen but not the spend, so the only safe grant was looking. Consequence to accept explicitly: a client cannot add its own users at all in V1, so every client user arrives by agency invite. Reveal obeys the same dependency rule as the others and cannot be granted without the Lead Finder screen.

D6 – Mailboxes are bought in the master, land unassigned, are assigned to one workspace, and return to the agency when the client leaves. A mailbox someone connected because they already owned it goes where they go (22 §6.2). Why: the first question an agency asks before moving its estate is what happens to the mailboxes it paid for when a client walks. Cost: this reverses the earlier position that a move is out of V1 (07 §6, 08 §6). Consequence to accept explicitly: whether warm-up and reputation survive the move is OQ4. If not, the action ships as "release and reconnect" and is not sold as a move. In V1 the only "client-bought" mailbox is one the client connected; a client workspace has no billing, so anything purchased is the agency's (24 §2.2).

D7 – One pool at the account, a cap per workspace; caps may exceed the pool, reservations may not. A new workspace gets a suggested cap and no reservation; Bifurcated is reservation equal to cap (07 §4, §5). Why: it is the ask on record, and the only mechanic that keeps oversubscription available while making a promise enforceable. Consequence to accept explicitly: the pool can empty while every workspace is under its cap ("starved"), and several clients stop at once. That state is loud on the agency side by requirement (§6.4). How Hemanshu's "distribute and bifurcate" decision maps onto this is OQ2.

D8 – Invite-and-accept replaces forwarded credentials. The client sets its own password; the agency never holds it (16 §6). Why: today the agency holds the client's password and the client consents to nothing. Consequence to accept explicitly: every existing client user gets one forced password reset on launch.

D9 – Migration, when it ships, moves pointers, not data. Each client record becomes a workspace; sequences and mailboxes follow their association; prospects follow their sequence; anything on no client or on two goes to a review queue, defaulting to "keep in agency"; rollback for 30 days (assumed, position in 08 §9). Why: the precedent moved sequences empty and made clients reconnect integrations (01 §7). Consequence to accept explicitly: a prospect on two clients' live sequences is the one case launch day cannot resolve without changing a running campaign. Whether that case exists in real data decides OQ1, and it is Hemanshu's and Dhruv's call, not this document's.

D10 – During the agency's payment grace period, a created client workspace stays readable and stops sending. The client's paused state names the agency and never the reason; the agency sees the failed payment, the days left, and every client as reading but not sending. Linked workspaces are on their own plan and are untouched. Why: in Aug 2025 an agency's failed payment locked its clients out until the 7-day grace period was patched (card 8972332385); a workspace model multiplies that surface, so the behaviour is decided rather than inherited. Decided 12 Sep 2026.

D11 – On a linked workspace, "View as client" exists only if the client granted it. The grant is a switch on the link-approval card, off by default, and can be changed later from the client's Workspace screen. A created workspace is the agency's and needs no grant. Why: the client funds and owns a linked workspace; nothing in the link request asked for the right to impersonate them. Decided 12 Sep 2026.

6.4 Limits, allocation and usage

For: owner (and manager, without cost columns) setting caps; client reading its own. A7 allocation, B1 plan usage, client at limit.

Must do: per workspace per resource, a Limit and a Reserved value; the pool shows Unallocated. Edits are drafted and applied with an after-state ("Limits total 116% → 124%", illustrative) rather than committed per keystroke. 6.6 Roll-ups and the Dashboard

9. Implementation notes

The mechanism that makes §6.7 possible is the prospect gaining a workspace dimension. Everything else is scoping over resources that already carry a client id. Stated as outcome only: a prospect belongs to exactly one workspace; the same email in two workspaces is two prospects; the agency sees the fact and count of a collision through Pattern B and never the other record (08 §7). Existing prospects are assigned by their sequence association at conversion; the rest enter M2.

The roll-up safety mechanism: membership filter applied on the server for every cross-client list, and a binding on every row that fixes the write target: the sending mailbox for a reply, the workspace id on a task or call record. Existing API consumers sending no workspace are pinned to the account's own-outreach workspace so nothing breaks silently (08 §12).

Resolve before estimation. An estimate against any of these is wrong, not approximate.
