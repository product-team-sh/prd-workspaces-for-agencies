1. Problem

What users do. An agency runs several clients' outreach from one account. It tags each sequence and each mailbox with a client, the only two client-assignable resources (agency-client.ts:7), issues each client a login at /agency/login, and forwards the credentials by hand (01 §1). Every agency user sees every client. Credits, sends and prospects are one pool with no client dimension (01 §5).

What actually happens. The prospect is account-level, so a client cannot be shown its own prospect's email trail without exposing every other client's activity; the sidebar is hidden and the agency screenshares (01 §6a). A sequence's client cannot be changed after activation, so agencies delete and recreate the client (01 §6). No client can be capped, so one client can empty the pool for the rest (Rajveersingh, 10 Aug 2026). Two agencies (measured) that asked for a restricted client view got hardcoded account ids in user-details.tsx, checked in 19 files (measured).

Why they do not find out. Nothing tells an agency that a client login is a filter over shared data. The client dimension exists in five states across modules (measured, 01 §6). When the pool empties, a client's sending stops with no message naming the agency.

What the fix creates. A solo operator with two clients moves from one screen to three contexts (24 §1.2). If migration is deferred (OQ1), two client models run at once (23 §7.4). "Workspace" already means "team" in plan copy (OQ15). Caps that sum past the pool mean several clients can stop at once.

2. Why this approach

Decision: a container per client, one account pool with a cap per workspace, a role per person, and the ordinary product inside each container.

CandidateRejected on
Patch the label model: client filter on Prospects and Tasks, per-client capsIsolation and client visibility are mutually exclusive while the prospect is account-level (01 §6a); the hardcoded ids are what patching looks like at scale
A subscription per workspace (Instantly)Contradicts the live "Unlimited Clients" claim on all three tiers ([true,true,true], measured)
Sub-wallets: credits moved out of the poolStrands capacity and forbids the oversubscription agency margin depends on (07 §4.2)

Not claimed: any competitor's isolation guarantee, which is marketing copy we could not test (03). The competitive bar is named roles plus membership per client and a capped shared pool; nobody verifiable ships a per-screen editor for clients (51 §4).

3. What we're building

  1. Nothing changes until the user adds a workspace. The account is the ordinary product with one line under the onboarding checklist; adding a workspace turns the account into the admin panel plus workspaces, and the old account becomes the user's own workspace with everything it had.
  2. A workspace is the ordinary product, scoped. Its sequences, prospects, mailboxes, settings, integrations and people belong to it; Billing is replaced by Usage; nothing in one workspace is readable from another.
  3. One pool, a cap per workspace, a role per person. Every consumable is metered to the workspace that spent it; reaching a cap stops that resource in that workspace and names the account; Owner and Admin reach every workspace, a Member only the workspaces they are on; a workspace user holds one of three roles in one workspace.

Invariant in every state: a workspace user never sees another workspace's name, record or count; every client-facing limit message names the account; sending never stops mid-step for a prospect in flight; money is visible only on the account's Billing; a client login cannot be left with zero screens.

5. Decisions

Rationale for D1 to D14 is in the v1 archive; one line each here so nothing is re-litigated.

  • D1 – Each client is its own workspace; workspaces never share data. The same person in two workspaces is two records; the account sees a collision count, never the other record.
  • D2 – The admin panel administers; the account's own outreach is a workspace of its own. Amended by D13; the split is what the prototype shows.
  • D3 – Numbers roll up (Analytics); records never do; row roll-ups (Unified Inbox, Tasks) are phase 2.
  • D4 – Six fixed roles. Account: Owner, Admin, Member. Workspace: Workspace Admin, Collaborator, Viewer. The table in §6.6 is the contract. No per-screen editor, preset or Custom state in phase 1.
  • D5 – A workspace user's role is their only permission, set per person, one workspace per user. Full access becomes Workspace Admin, Limited becomes Collaborator, No access becomes Viewer.
  • D6 – Mailboxes are bought inside the workspace that uses them and belong to it from purchase. No pool, assignment or move in phase 1.
  • D7 – One pool; per workspace and per resource a cap and an optional reservation. A cap can exceed the pool, a reservation cannot. Amended 30 Sep 2026: no Bifurcated mode and no independent budget in phase 1 (row 5). Consequence to accept explicitly: every workspace draws from the shared pool; the pool can run out while every workspace is under cap, and Billing must say so.
  • D8 – A client sets its own password; the account never sees it.
  • D9 – Migration moves pointers, not data; 30-day rollback (assumed). Ships only if OQ1 resolves to "with V1".
  • D10 – During the account's payment grace period a workspace stays readable and stops sending; the client's copy names the account, never the reason.
  • D11 – Phase 2. View as client on a linked workspace needs the client's grant.
  • D12 – Analytics across workspaces is phase 1; API & Integrations on the admin panel is phase 2; inside each workspace the existing API & Integrations section stays, scoped to it; warm-up stays one TrulyInbox account per account.
  • D13 – The admin panel is Home, Workspaces, People, Billing, Analytics; Work queue, Unified Inbox, Tasks, Email Accounts, Templates and API & Integrations are phase 2, shown as Coming soon. Amended 30 Sep 2026 by D15: the "no Settings on the admin panel" clause is withdrawn.
  • D14 – The product says workspaces, not clients. "Client" only where it means the outside party: View as client, the sign-in orientation line, the migration screen. Amended 30 Sep 2026: the "who is this workspace for" question is not asked in phase 1 (row 3, OQ20).

D15 – Settings is on the admin panel and shows the account level. Reverses the Settings clause of D13. The rail foot is the product's: Settings, Submit Feedback, Need support. On the admin panel Settings opens on My Profile; account items (Users & Teams, Admin Settings, Whitelabel, Billing & Subscription) are set there; workspace-shaped items (Prospect Fields, Custom Tracking Domain, Do Not Contact and Do Not Call lists) read "Each workspace, its own copy" and are set inside the workspace. Consequence to accept explicitly: a setting with an account default and a workspace override has two places it can be changed; the workspace's own copy always shows its source (§6.8).

D16 – The rail is the product's rail, flat, and the switcher is the tile at its foot. Reverses v1 §6.1 (two-group switcher under the logo) and the grouped rail. Adding a workspace adds items to the rail (Home, Workspaces, People, Billing, Analytics on the account; Usage in a workspace); it never adds a heading or a second navigation. A workspace user's tile is static. Consequence to accept explicitly: the way from a client workspace back to the admin panel is the switcher's account row; only the account's own workspace carries a Back to account item (OQ21).

D17 – Every workspace is added with its limits reviewed. Reverses v1 §6.2's skippable caps step and voids CL2. Add a workspace is Details then Limits; Limits lists every resource the plan holds with a suggested cap per row; Add is on step 2. Consequence to accept explicitly: adding a workspace is two screens, not one; a workspace can never exist with unreviewed caps, so the "limits pending" checklist state of v1 does not exist.

D18 – Requests and limit changes are decided on Billing > Workspace limits & usage and on the record's Limits tab, not on Home. Reverses the "Home keeps the decisions, acted on in place" clause of D13; voids LM3 and LM4 as written. Home's status line names what waits ("8 decisions are waiting", illustrative) and links to Billing. Consequence to accept explicitly: an Owner reads Home, then goes one screen further to act; there is no in-place approve on Home in phase 1.

D19 – Home is four hero cards and the workspaces table, nothing else. Workspaces, Active sequences, Outreach this cycle, Replies this cycle; the table keeps its columns in every state, with a Setup pending chip for a workspace that has steps left. Consequence to accept explicitly: plan pacing and pooled-resource runway live only on Billing; a fresh account's Home is mostly empty until a workspace sends.

D20 – The workspace record administers: Overview, Members, Limits. Its Email Accounts and Settings tabs are Coming soon in phase 1; mailboxes and settings are managed inside the workspace (Open workspace, one click). Consequence to accept explicitly: an Owner who wants to see a workspace's mailbox health from the record goes into the workspace; the read-only record view is phase 2.

9. Implementation notes

The mechanism behind §6.8 is the prospect gaining a workspace dimension. Everything else is scoping over resources that already carry a client id. Outcome only: a prospect belongs to exactly one workspace; the same email in two workspaces is two prospects; the account sees the fact and count of a collision, never the other record. Existing prospects are assigned by their sequence at conversion; the rest enter the review. Existing API consumers sending no workspace are pinned to the account's own workspace (08 §12).
