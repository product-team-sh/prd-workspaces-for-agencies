Part 1. The product in ten minutes

1.1 The problem

An agency runs several clients from one account. A client is a label on sequences and mailboxes, the only two client-assignable resources (agency-client.ts:7); prospects, tasks, credits and sends have no client dimension (01 §5). So a client cannot see its own prospect's trail without seeing every other client's, no client can be capped, every agency user sees every client, and the two agencies that asked for a restricted client view got hardcoded account ids checked in 19 files (measured, user-details.tsx). Nothing tells the agency any of this; the client finds out when sending stops with no message naming the agency.

1.3 What a workspace is and the three things it changes

Nothing changes until the user adds a workspace. The account is the ordinary product. Adding a workspace turns it into an admin panel (Home, Workspaces, People, Billing, Analytics) plus workspaces; the old account becomes the user's own workspace with everything it had, and deleting the only other workspace puts it back. A workspace is the ordinary product, scoped. It changes three things.

  1. Where data lives. Sequences, prospects, mailboxes, tasks, inbox, templates, settings, integrations, API keys and people belong to the workspace, one set per workspace instead of one for the account; Billing is replaced by Usage; nothing in one workspace is readable from another. Settings follow 38: account items one copy, workspace-shaped items a copy per workspace with an account default and lock, and no Settings on the admin panel. The prospect gains a workspace dimension, so the same email in two workspaces is two prospects.
  2. Who sees what. Owner and Admin reach every workspace and the admin panel; a Member only the workspaces on their list; a client's person holds one of three workspace roles (Workspace Admin, Collaborator, Viewer) on one or more workspaces, is invited and sets their own password, and never sees the agency, other clients, prices or the plan. Users and Teams become account roles with workspace membership; Team Manager retires into Member plus Admin.
  3. How the plan is shared. One pool, one limit per workspace per resource, credits reserved at the limit. Every consumable is metered to the workspace that spent it; a limit stops that resource in that workspace and names the account; limits never total more than the plan; a client asks for more from Usage and the agency decides on the workspace's record.

Invariant in every state: a workspace user never sees another workspace's name, record or count; every client-facing limit message names the account; sending never stops mid-step for a prospect in flight; money shows only on the account's Billing; a client login cannot be left with zero screens.

1.5 Six screens

The switcher. The tile at the foot of the rail answers "where am I" and "where else can I go". Owner and Admin see the admin panel, their own workspace and every client; a Member only the workspaces on their list; a client user on one workspace sees their workspace's name and nothing clickable. Rows carry one attention tag at most, no counts and no roles.

Home. Where the Owner and Admin land: three cards (workspaces, active sequences, outreach this cycle) and one table with a row per workspace, columns in the order of the owner's questions. Everything is this billing cycle to date against last cycle to the same day. Red cells carry the attention; no status column, no date picker.

The workspace record. Where a workspace is administered, not reported: Overview (setup, access, limits, details), Members, Limits and Activity. Limits is the decision screen: a client's ask is approved or declined line by line, and each limit is a number beside its resource with what the plan has available under it. Disable, Delete (restorable for 30 days) and View as client sit in the header.

People. Everyone who can sign in, in one list: the agency's team with their account role and the workspaces they reach, and each client's people with their workspace and role. Invite people reads each address's domain and proposes who the person is; every row opens a person page that is the invite card they were invited with, editable, plus their activity across every workspace they reach.

Billing. Money, Owner only: the next bill, the plan, where the plan goes (one read-only bar per metered resource, split by workspace), seats and add-ons, credits to buy. Nothing here changes a limit; limits are set on each workspace's Limits tab.

The client's view. A client user opens their workspace and sees the ordinary product with Usage in place of Billing. Usage says whether the outreach keeps running this month, shows each allowance as used of limit, names the agency as the one who set it, and lets a Workspace Admin or Collaborator ask for more. Nothing of the agency, other clients, prices or the plan appears anywhere.
