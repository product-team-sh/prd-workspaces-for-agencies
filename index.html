1. Overview

What a workspace is. Nothing changes until the user adds a workspace. Adding one turns the account into an admin panel (Home, Workspaces, People, Billing, Analytics, Whitelabel) plus workspaces; the old account becomes the user's own workspace with everything it had, and deleting the only other workspace puts it back. A workspace is the ordinary product, scoped; Billing is replaced by Usage; nothing in one workspace is readable from another. Copy says "workspace"; "client" is only the outside party.

Invariant in every state: a workspace user never sees another workspace's name, record or count; every client-facing limit message names the account; sending never stops mid-step for a prospect in flight; money shows only on the account's Billing; a client login cannot be left with zero screens.

The three things it changes.

  1. Where data lives. One set of sequences, prospects, mailboxes, tasks, inbox, templates, settings, integrations, API keys and people per workspace. The prospect gains a workspace dimension: the same email in two workspaces is two prospects; the account sees only the fact and count of a collision. Account-level settings have one copy; workspace-shaped settings a copy per workspace with an account default and lock.
  2. Who sees what. Agency users (Owner, Admin, Member) live on the account; client users (Workspace Admin, Collaborator, Viewer) on one or more client workspaces. Owner and Admin reach every workspace; a Member only those on their list, with a workspace role on each; a client user never sees the agency, other clients, prices or the plan.
  3. How the plan is shared. One pool; one limit per workspace per resource; credits reserved at the limit. Every consumable is metered to the workspace that spent it; a limit stops that resource there and names the account; limits never total more than the plan; a client asks for more from Usage, the agency decides on the record.

2. Rules

2.1 People and roles

Two kinds of person exist on an account; the kind is fixed at invite and never changes.

KindRolesWhere they liveWhat they are for
Agency/Admin Account userOwner, Admin, MemberThe account. Owner and Admin reach every workspace; a Member reaches the workspaces on their list, with a workspace role on eachRunning the agency and its clients' outreach
Client userWorkspace Admin, Collaborator, ViewerOne or more client workspaces, a role on eachWorking in, or watching, their own outreach

Rules:

  • One email is one kind. An agency user cannot be invited as a client user, or the reverse; the invite form refuses with "[email] is already on this account as [kind]".
  • A client user can be on more than one workspace, a role on each, on the same sign-in. A client cannot add its own users; invites are the account's.
  • A Member carries a workspace role per workspace, the same three as a client user, picked at invite and editable on the person page. Inside a workspace a Member sees the whole workspace (every sequence, prospect and reply there, including colleagues' work) and acts at that workspace's role.
  • Owner and Admin are Workspace Admin everywhere, automatically.
  • Default role at invite: Viewer for a client's person; Workspace Admin for an agency-domain teammate.
  • Delete a workspace is Owner only; Disable is Owner and Admin.
  • Each invitee sets their own password.
  • Only the Owner sees money: the bill, the plan, where the plan goes, seats, invoices, mailbox purchases, the grace-period "Update payment". An Admin sees allowances and limits as quantities, never prices. A client user sees quantities set by the agency and nothing about cost.

2.3 Limits and asks

Six resources are limits an agency can share out; they are the rows of every Limits surface (the creation share, the record's Limits tab, Billing's Where your plan goes, the in-workspace Usage page). Plan values as on the public pricing page:

ResourceStarter / Pro / ScaleKindAt creationReserved? (always equal to the limit)
Emails sent / month6,000 / 100,000 / 200,000 (Scale tiers to 600,000)Renews monthlyshare of the planNo, a limit only
Lead Finder credits / month1,500 / 4,000 / 8,000Renews monthlyshare of the planYes
Email verification credits500 / 1,000 / 5,000, plus purchasesOne-time balanceshare of the balanceYes
AI credits100 / 500 / 1,000, plus purchasesOne-time balanceshare of the balanceYes
Dialer creditsadd-on, purchasedAdd-on balanceshare of the balance, only when the account holds oneYes
Inbox placement testsadd-on, purchasedAdd-on balanceshare of the balance, only when the account holds oneYes

Rules:

  • Limits are fixed numbers. For a credit the reservation always equals the limit, so no Reserved column exists anywhere; for emails the number is a monthly limit. No reserve switch, no bifurcated mode, no independent budget.
  • "Available", on every screen, is the plan minus every limit already given. A workspace can always use up to its own limit. Limits never total more than the plan: an edit that would take them over is refused naming both numbers, and an ask is approved only when the plan has the amount available; an ask that does not fit cannot be approved until another workspace is lowered or capacity is added, and the reason sits under that line.
  • Where a number is set. At creation every number comes from one share of the plan (3.2 step 4); afterwards each is edited on the workspace's Limits tab, or all at once through Change the share. Billing changes nothing.
  • Where an ask surfaces: Home ("Needs a decision"), the record header chip, the Limits tab bar and Activity. No bell list in phase 1.
  • Not limits, so never shown as one: prospect storage, email accounts, users and teams (Unlimited on every plan), active prospects (Unlimited on Scale; a plan limit on Starter and Pro only, and agencies are gated to Scale, 2.4). A share of a one-time balance does not renew: reserving 10% of 5,000 verification credits takes 500 from a pot that refills only when bought.
  • Payment grace: every workspace readable, none sending; the client's copy names the account, never the reason; the brand and the client login stay.

3. Screens

Conventions. Loading: every screen paints its own skeleton (its shapes in Gray 100, the design system's loading token) until its data arrives, then fades in; no spinner, no blank page; a tab switch inside a screen does not re-skeleton the screen; reduced motion shows the skeleton still but without the shimmer or the fade. Priorities on test cases: P0 blocks release, P1 blocks GA, P2 follows. States: Fresh is an account that just added its first workspace (own workspace plus one new one); Partial has workspaces at different stages; Running has five client workspaces. In the per-role sentences, "edit" means the person can change it, "read" means they see it and cannot, "no" means the screen or element is absent for them.
